# FY 2569 S-band radar price reasonableness review

*Review date: 17 September 2026. This is a screening analysis, not a
forensic cost audit or a finding of overpricing.*

## Bottom line

The four awarded prices are not facially excessive against the published
budget or the e-GP reference price: every award is below both benchmarks.
However, price reasonableness is not fully established because the lowest
bidder failed consideration in all four tenders and the public portal does
not disclose the technical/qualification reasons. The strongest defensible
conclusion is therefore: **award prices look budget-disciplined, but the
underlying price competition and cost build-up remain unverified.**

## Provisional answer to “ควรอยู่ที่เท่าไหร่?”

For a **full turnkey package comparable to these four projects**—S-band
dual-polarization radar, high-power transmitter, antenna/radome, tower or
building works, communications, installation, testing and initial warranty—my
evidence-based screening band is:

| Band | Indicative price | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Lower plausible bound | **390–400M baht** | Requires a genuinely compliant offer and relatively light site/civil scope. |
| Most defensible central band | **400–420M baht** | Consistent with the eight prices submitted by bidders who passed consideration; their median is 412.421M. |
| Needs specific justification | **420–446M baht** | Could be justified by difficult site works, higher lifecycle/support scope, or different equipment; needs BOQ evidence. |
| Above 446.167M | **High-risk zone** | Exceeds the displayed reference price and should require an item-by-item explanation. |

This is a **reasonable procurement-price range**, not the manufacturer's
internal production cost. The internal cost cannot be inferred from public
award prices without the offered model, import price, OEM discount, local
installation cost, warranty reserve and supplier margin.

The newly archived RRD source PDFs sharpen the screen for the separate FY2570
Udon/Phimai proposals. Their quotation pages show radar-core lines of
422.770M (Scientific Research) and 427.000M (Marwin) at both sites, while
Genomatch quotes 480M as a single bundled line. The radar-core spread between
the two itemized firms is only 4.230M (about 1.0%), and the total spread across
the three firms is 12M. This is not a normalized cost estimate: the quotes
allocate buildings, solar, road/fence, and drones differently, and Genomatch's
62.2156M included-scope note is not itemized in the same way. It does,
however, make the underlying BOQs and quotation-request trail high-priority
audit documents.

On that basis, **400M is not obviously expensive**. It sits at the lower end
of the observed qualified-bidder market and is close to the 394.295M RRD
Rong Khwang S-band contract, although that is only a directional comparison
because the scope and procurement method differ.

Two observations are material:

1. Among the two bidders marked passed, the selected price is only 0.28–1.03%
   below the other passing price. This does not look like a large price
   concession within the qualified set.
2. The rejected lowest offers were 4.71–9.54% below the selected price. If
   the technical exclusions are fully justified, the selected prices may be
   reasonable for compliant systems. If not, the portal data alone cannot
   explain why the state paid materially more than the lowest offers.

## Historical and adjacent benchmarks

These are directional only because radar band, tower, civil works,
connectivity, transmitter, warranty, and delivery scope differ:

- TMD FY 2561: new S-band Doppler radar with communications equipment and
  tower, 304.950M baht. The FY 2569 awards are 34.1–36.1% higher in nominal
  terms, but the eight-year gap and unnormalized specifications prevent a
  real-terms overpricing conclusion.
- TMD FY 2556: two Dual Polarization radars with accessories and towers,
  308.995M total, approximately 154.5M per unit. This is a different era,
  package and likely band/scope; it is not a valid direct unit-price test.
- RRD FY 2564: Rong Khwang S-band Dual-Pol radar with building,
  394.295M baht by specific/direct method. This is closer in platform class,
  but the procurement agency, specification, civil scope and method differ.
- RRD FY 2570: the Budget Bureau's draft agriculture budget now confirms
  named 480M multi-year entries for Phimai and Udon Thani, with 72M in FY
  2570 and 192M/216M in the following two years. They remain draft-budget
  entries, not awarded prices. They are 15.6–17.4% above the FY 2569 TMD
  awards, but cannot be used as proof of excess without the final scope and
  BOQ.
- RRD supplied quotations: the original Drive PDFs now confirm the three
  quoted totals at both sites (480M / 486.3M / 492M), all dated 19 June 2569.
  Itemized radar lines are 422.770M and 427.000M before differently allocated
  site/building/add-on components. These are quotation figures, not awards or
  manufacturer's costs.
- PBO-MCP GFMIS query: the FY2558–2568 disbursement dataset contains no FY2568
  line matching Phimai/Rangka Yai, Udon, or Mueang Mon. Historical Phimai
  matches are small protection-equipment/CCTV items, not the proposed radar
  replacement. This is useful context but cannot validate the 480M proposal:
  PBO is a disbursement export and stops at FY2568.

### New domestic TMD budget benchmarks

The additional TMD subcommittee document provides a closer internal screen,
but it also prevents a misleading comparison. Its 480M “Aviation Hub” line is
not one radar: it is a multi-site aviation-weather package covering eight
airports plus Suvarnabhumi central integration, with LiDAR, lightning warning,
electric-field sensors, software, servers, network/security and power
equipment. It should be removed from any list of “480M radar” projects.

The same document proposes **210M for one fixed C-band dual-polarization radar
at Ang Thong**, including communications, tower, generator/storage buildings,
power equipment, spares and training. It also proposes **160M for two mobile
X-band radars**, or 80M per vehicle as a simple average. These are budget
proposals, not awards, and they are not technically identical to the RRD
fixed S-band packages. Nevertheless, the 210M Ang Thong proposal is the most
relevant Thai internal benchmark found so far: it makes the RRD itemized
radar-core indications of about 422.770–427M a high-priority question for
technical normalization.

The domestic comparison currently looks like this:

| Package | Scope | Proposed/awarded amount | Use in screening |
|---|---|---:|---|
| TMD Ang Thong | 1 fixed C-band dual-pol radar + link + tower/buildings | 210M proposed | Closest domestic turnkey benchmark; partial comparability |
| TMD mobile X-band | 2 mobile dual-pol radar vehicles | 160M proposed | Mobile benchmark only; 80M/unit arithmetic average |
| TMD FY2569 S-band | 1 fixed S-band dual-pol package per station | 408.954–415.160M awarded | Same broad class, but its 750-kW/other TOR envelope differs from RRD |
| RRD Udon/Phimai | 1 fixed S-band package per site | 480M proposed; quotes 480–492M | Needs BOQ and technical justification |

The TMD document also supplies a useful replacement-rationale comparator:
the 50M Buriram AWOS request explicitly cites frequent failures, obsolete
components and discontinued parts. RRD's Phimai replacement case should be
supported with equivalent asset-condition evidence for the METEOR 600S.

The same PDF contains the underlying three-quote sets for the two radar-related
benchmarks:

| TMD package | Lowest quote | Other quotes | Proposed budget |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Ang Thong fixed C-band | Scientific Research 210.000M | Genomatch 231.200M; Marwin 254.650M | 210M |
| Two mobile X-band vehicles | Scientific Research 160.000M | Marwin 169.600M; Genomatch 174.400M | 160M |

In both cases the proposed budget equals the lowest quotation in the PDF. This
does not establish wrongdoing—the quotes may have been requested specifically
for budget formulation—but it is a directly documented “budget = lowest quote”
pattern. It also means the 210M Ang Thong figure should be described as both a
budget and the lowest of three visible supplier quotes, rather than as an
independent market price. The Aviation Hub package shows the same structure:
AsiaMet 480M, Marwin 508.176M and Excel 522M, with a 480M budget.

There is a further comparability warning: the visible quotations for Aviation
Hub, Ang Thong and mobile X-band each state one-year warranty, whereas the
corresponding TMD project/TOR text states two years. Before treating the
lowest quote as a valid price for the requested package, obtain any
clarification or revised quotation that reconciles this warranty difference.

The one-page quote sheets also omit model numbers and line-item BOQs. Marwin’s
Ang Thong sheet names EEC as the brand, but the visible Scientific and
Genomatch sheets do not identify a model. The price spreads therefore cannot
yet be explained by transmitter, antenna, vehicle, software, civil works or
support differences.

The spread in these visible TMD quotation sets is also informative: Aviation
Hub spans 480–522M (8.75% from lowest to highest), Ang Thong C-band spans
210–254.65M (21.26%), and mobile X-band spans 160–174.4M (9.00%). By
comparison, the RRD Udon/Phimai quotation totals span only 480–492M (2.50%)
at each site. Different scopes and request conditions may explain the
difference, so this is a scrutiny lead—not evidence of coordinated pricing.

## International benchmarks

The strongest public foreign price comparator found is an official PAGASA
Notice of Award for Hinatuan, Philippines. It awarded an S-band magnetron-type
dual-polarization Doppler weather radar and accessories at PHP 190.345M. At an
indicative 16 September 2026 rate of 0.5301 THB/PHP, that is approximately
100.9M baht ([official Notice of Award](https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/pagasaweb/files/procurement/IB%202025-50%20NP_NOA_Enhanced%20-%20Signed_02182026%20Edited.pdf)). PAGASA's FY2024 indicative APP separately budgeted PHP 200.000M
for an Iloilo S-band dual-polarization magnetron weather radar with accessories,
approximately 106.0M baht at the same rate ([official APP](https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/pagasaweb/files/transparency/PAGASA%20Indicative%20APP%202024.pdf)). The latter is a budget, not an award.

On the face of the published figures, the Thai awards at 408.954–415.160M are
about 3.8–4.1 times the two Philippine figures. This is a warning signal, not a
like-for-like overpricing finding. The Philippine award notice does not expose a
line-item BOQ. More importantly, the Hinatuan bid bulletin says the generator,
automatic transfer switch and air-conditioning are under a separate building
construction scope, and that the radar contractor coordinates with a separate
building contractor for the tower/pedestal/radome interface ([official Hinatuan
bulletin](https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/pagasaweb/files/procurement/SBB%20IB%202025-50-01_HInatuan.pdf)). The Thai TOR, by contrast, includes an 11m-or-larger
radome, a new radar-tower building, 100 kVA generator, UPS systems, site works,
communications and related buildings. The exchange conversion also does not
normalize taxes, duties, financing, delivery terms or local construction costs.

At the equipment level, the comparison is directionally relevant: both records
identify S-band, dual-polarization, Doppler weather radar and magnetron as an
available/required transmitter technology. It is not the same complete package.
The Thai TOR also sets a 750 kW peak-power floor; the public Hinatuan award
notice and bulletin reviewed here do not disclose enough numeric radar
parameters to confirm equal peak power, antenna size, range or receiver
performance.

Two other official foreign records help define the comparison boundary but do
not provide a usable price: [India's IMD tender](https://internal.imd.gov.in/tenders/20250510_td_313.pdf)
is for eight S-band polarimetric Doppler systems with three years of warranty
followed by seven years of CAMC; [Pakistan's PMD notice](https://circular.pmd.gov.pk/new/assets/tenders/tender-supply%2C-installation-and-civil-works-of-fixed-weather-surveillance-radars-with-power-backup-system-2025-03-20-10-59.pdf)
describes an S-band magnetron system at 850 kW or more, 450 km range, with dual
polarization, a 30 kVA generator and battery backup. [PAGASA's own clarification](https://pubfiles.pagasa.dost.gov.ph/pagasaweb/files/procurement/SBB%20IB%202026-24-01%20SGD.pdf)
for a comparable tender also says that a solid-state transmitter offer is
non-compliant where the specification requires magnetron.
This confirms that transmitter technology and support/site scope must be
normalized before comparing prices.

### Additional countries with public numbers

- **China:** Zhoukou's official procurement result records CNY 14.500M for an
  S-band dual-line-polarization Doppler weather radar. At an indicative
  16 September 2026 rate of 4.98 THB/CNY, this is approximately **72.2M baht**.
  The public result does not disclose the model or whether tower, civil works,
  installation and warranty are included, so it is an equipment/scope-uncertain
  comparator ([official award notice](https://www.ccgp.gov.cn/cggg/zygg/cjgg/202605/t20260518_26585111.htm)).
- **South Africa:** SAWS published three received bids for a turnkey S-band
  dual-polarized weather radar in Durban: ZAR 54.760M, 62.855M and 66.522M,
  approximately **111.7M, 128.2M and 135.7M baht** at 2.04 THB/ZAR. This is
  the closest current turnkey comparison found, but it is a bid-spread record,
  not a published award ([official bid record](https://www.etenders.gov.za/Home/Download/?blobName=96b74600-291a-408d-9a01-0612c4d16ba8.pdf&downloadedFileName=BID++SAWS+391c+Publication+of+received+bids.pdf)).
- **Canada:** ECCC reports a CAD 180.4M contract to buy and install 33 S-band
  dual-polarization radars, or approximately CAD 5.467M / **130.7M baht per
  radar** at 23.91 THB/CAD. The Canadian published system uses a 9.15m antenna,
  11.8m radome, 330km conventional range and 240km Doppler range. This is a
  2016 program price, so it is nominal and benefits from 33-unit standardization,
  but it is still a useful system-class benchmark ([Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/weather-general-tools-resources/radar-overview/radar-replacement-project.html)).

Together with the Philippines figure of approximately 100.9M baht, the public
foreign observations with numbers cluster broadly around **72–136M baht per
system**, before normalizing scope, inflation, taxes and site works. The Thai
awards remain 3–6 times that nominal band. Canada and South Africa are not
perfect matches, but they make the Thai price gap materially harder to dismiss
as merely a regional construction-cost difference.

**International-price conclusion:** the foreign evidence changes the question,
but does not by itself prove “400M is four times too expensive.” The foreign
evidence now supports a stronger preliminary finding: **the Thai price is a
high-risk outlier that requires a detailed cost build-up**. The next valid test
is to strip the Thai contract into radar equipment, radome, tower/building,
power, communications, civil works and support, then compare each line with the
foreign scope. If the Thai BOQ shows less than roughly 70M baht for building,
civil works and site systems, the residual radar-system price is still more
than 330M baht—well above the public foreign system-class observations and in
need of a specific technical/OEM explanation.

## Assessment

| Question | Assessment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Is the award below the agency's budget? | **Yes** | All four are 2.32–3.78% below 425M. |
| Is it below the stated reference price? | **Yes** | All four are 6.95–8.34% below 446.166667M. |
| Does the qualified-bidder comparison show a large overpay? | **Not on its face** | Winners are 0.28–1.03% below the other passing bid. |
| Does the lowest rejected price establish overpricing? | **No** | Its technical/qualification failure is unexplained publicly. |
| Is the reference price independently validated? | **Not yet** | TMD's reference price is identical across the four pages; the newly found RRD quotations concern a separate FY2570 package and do not validate the TMD reference price. |
| Can historical prices prove overpricing? | **No** | The available comparisons are not scope- or inflation-normalized. |
| Does the new TMD budget PDF prove the RRD 480M price is excessive? | **No, but it raises the priority of the question** | TMD's 210M C-band package is a useful domestic comparator, but band, transmitter, range, tower/building, site, communications and support scope are not yet normalized. |

## Highest-value documents for a real price audit

1. The three original reference-price quotations and TMD request letters for
   each station, with dates and line-item BOQ.
2. Final TOR, offered model numbers, compliance matrices, tower/civil works,
   connectivity, warranty and maintenance terms.
3. Technical-evaluation reports explaining the four lowest-price exclusions.
4. Bid-opening records and any clarification requests/responses.
5. Signed contracts and payment/variation records to check whether the award
   price remained the final economic cost.
