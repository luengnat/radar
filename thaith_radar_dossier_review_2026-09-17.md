# ThaiTH.AI radar dossier review — 17 September 2026

Source reviewed: [ThaiTH.AI — ตรวจงบเรดาร์ 2570](https://open.thaith.ai/radar/).

This page is a useful investigative index, not a primary government record. It
explicitly says that its documents and map were produced with AI assistance and
must be checked against the originals. The observations below are therefore
split into leads, corroboration, and unresolved points.

## What the page adds

### 1. It is a different procurement scope from the TMD FY2569 awards

The dossier examines eight FY2570 requests from two agencies, totalling
2,498.8M baht. Its main radar issue is the Royal Rainmaking Department's two
requests:

| Request | Agency | Amount | Page's framing |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Udon Thani, Mueang Mon | Royal Rainmaking | 480.0M | New fixed S-band dual-pol station |
| Phimai | Royal Rainmaking | 480.0M | Replacement of METEOR 600S installed in 2555 |

These are not the four TMD FY2569 contracts already reconciled in this repo
(409.0–415.2M per station). The two investigations should remain separate.

### 2. The network-overlap argument is a strong document request, not yet proof

The page plots approximate coordinates and uses 240 km for fixed radar and
150 km for mobile radar. It claims that the proposed Udon site is within the
stated radii of Ban Phue RRD (42 km), Khon Kaen TMD (110 km), and Sakon Nakhon
TMD (147 km); Phimai is within Khon Kaen TMD (142 km) and Takhli RRD (231 km).

An official TMD weather page independently shows public feeds for Khon Kaen
(120/240 km), Sakon Nakhon, and the Phimai rainmaking radar. That corroborates
the existence of overlapping public radar feeds, but not that they provide the
same elevation coverage, uptime, beam geometry, data latency, or operational
authority needed for rainmaking.

The correct next test is an official coverage/gap analysis using exact
coordinates, terrain/beam-blockage data, availability by station, and a data-
sharing arrangement between TMD and RRD. The ThaiTH.AI map itself says its
coordinates are approximate and excludes military, aviation, airport, and
other networks.

### 3. The India benchmark is material but not a like-for-like Thai price

The dossier cites an IMD/Astra order of ₹171.38 crore for six Klystron-based
S-band polarimetric Doppler radars, or about ₹28.56 crore / 114.3M baht per
radar using the page's approximate conversion. The cited scope includes AWS,
disdrometers, three years' warranty, and seven years' comprehensive
maintenance.

IMD's official tender page confirms the relevant procurement class and scope
(eight S-band systems with AWS/disdrometers, three-year warranty and seven-year
CAMC), while the public award amount is reported through the company/news
record rather than the tender page itself. It is therefore a strong price
screen, not a final Thai benchmark: the Thai RRD package may include tower,
building, site works, solar/carport or other add-ons, while the India scope
and exchange-rate/date basis must be normalized first.

### 4. The most useful internal red flags are reproducible questions

The dossier says both RRD quotation sets total exactly 480.0M for Genomatch,
486.3M for Scientific Research, and 492.0M for Marwin, with the same date and
sequential-looking quotation identifiers. It also says the draft TOR tables
name “LEONARDO/Gematronik” and that Genomatch describes itself as the sole
Thai distributor.

These are high-value leads because the answer is documentary: obtain the
original TOR versions, the letters requesting quotations, the three complete
line-item quotes, the Pr.4/Pr.5/Pr.6 cost sheets, the brand-equivalence
justification, and the procurement committee minutes. The pattern alone is
not proof of collusion or unlawful conduct.

## What changes in our assessment

1. The RRD FY2570 Udon request now deserves priority as a “need/duplication”
   review: it is described as a new station despite nearby RRD and TMD feeds.
2. The Phimai request has a more plausible replacement rationale, but needs an
   OEM end-of-life/end-of-support letter and an independent condition report
   for the METEOR 600S before a 480M replacement can be assessed.
3. The India comparison strengthens the price question. It does not establish
   that 480M is excessive until the radar core, building, site works, add-ons,
   warranty, maintenance, tax, and installation are separated.
4. Nothing on this page changes the TMD FY2569 finding: TMD's final TORs were
   archived as brand-neutral, and the four awarded projects remain a separate
   1,647.8M procurement with unresolved written reasons for rejecting the four
   lowest-price offers.

## Highest-value requests generated by this review

1. RRD's final/signed TORs, complete BOQs, and the quotation-request trail for
   Udon and Phimai. The supplied PDFs now cover the scanned TOR and quotation
   pages, but do not by themselves establish final procurement status.
2. Exact station coordinates, terrain/coverage study, uptime logs, and the
   inter-agency radar-data MOU or explanation for its absence.
3. Phimai METEOR 600S asset history, service records, EOL notice, and
   replacement-versus-upgrade analysis.
4. The original IMD/Astra award notice or contract/BOQ, not only the reported
   aggregate amount.
5. Final RRD procurement method, e-GP project IDs, and any bid/technical
   evaluation records once the FY2570 procurements are announced.

## Tab-by-tab sweep

The interactive tabs add the following details beyond the landing summary:

- **Red flags:** the page groups its case into 12 cards. The strongest
  documentary leads are the printed brand line, same-day quotations, the
  unexplained radar-core amount, the absence of a Phimai EOL/condition report,
  and the absence of a visible price-committee appointment/order.
- **Technical filters:** the page says the draft TOR combines five restrictive
  layers: Magnetron at least 850 kW, a Thai-government experience threshold of
  at least 100M baht, manufacturer-developed software, a claimed “WMO
  certification”, and three-layer legalization of dealer documents. These are
  claims about the draft TOR; each clause should be checked against the signed
  source pages and the legal justification.
- **Construction and add-ons:** the visible calculation cards put the radar
  core at roughly 415–430M after deducting reported civil works and the Phimai
  drone package. They also identify reported extras such as a 10–12M solar
  carport, a 23.8–25M PM2.5 drone/command package, a 543,960-baht flagpole,
  and a 300-metre fence. These figures are useful targets for BOQ retrieval,
  not independently verified cost estimates.
- **Financial terms:** the draft terms shown are 15% advance payment (72M per
  station), a 45% first milestone, 840-day delivery, and two-year warranty.
  The page estimates post-warranty maintenance at 12–20M per station per year;
  that estimate needs a supplier/OEM service quote before use.
- **Quote network:** the page claims that 7 of 8 FY2570 requests equal their
  lowest quotation, while the earthquake-system request is the exception. It
  also claims that Genomatch, Scientific Research, and Marwin appear across
  both agencies. This is a pattern for document review, not evidence of
  coordinated bidding.
- **Reduction options:** its three scenarios are: cancel both RRD radars;
  keep only Phimai but re-price it at roughly 190–270M; or retain the package
  subject to at least 70M of immediate cuts and TOR/price-process conditions.
  These are advocacy scenarios, not official cost estimates.
- **Rebuttal tab:** the page correctly identifies its own weakest point: the
  India price is not like-for-like because the foreign scope, tax, logistics,
  tower/building, installation, and maintenance terms may differ. The stronger
  comparison is the domestic TMD C-band/X-band evidence already in the
  dossier, provided those internal price sheets are themselves reconciled.
- **Legal tab:** the page presents possible arguments under procurement,
  budget-discipline, information-access, and anti-bid-rigging laws. It also
  says explicitly that these are risk observations, not findings of criminal
  conduct. The legal tab should be treated as questions for counsel or the
  competent audit/investigative body, not as a legal conclusion.

The visible page does not itself expose the complete Udon/Phimai source bundle
as directly downloadable links. The user subsequently supplied a Drive folder
containing the two original 114-page scanned PDFs. That changes the status of
several claims below from secondary report to primary-source document
observation, while leaving final procurement status and evaluation reasons
unresolved.

## Primary-source check: user-supplied Drive originals

The two PDFs are [Udon Thani/Mueang Mon](data/sources_rrd/rrd_fy2570_udon_radar_original.pdf)
and [Phimai/Rangka Yai](data/sources_rrd/rrd_fy2570_phimai_radar_original.pdf),
also available in the [source Drive folder](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/170o8gB5Snc-Y96KNaNf5N4cpxSkLoFUn).
Both are 114-page scanned documents. Visual inspection of quotation pages
112–114 confirms that all six quotations are dated 19 June 2569 and that the
three totals repeat exactly at both sites:

| Quoting firm | Udon | Phimai | Key visible composition |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Genomatch | 480.000M | 480.000M | One 480M radar line; note says 62.2156M of building/site/add-on scope is included |
| Scientific Research | 486.300M | 486.300M | 422.770M radar at each site; remainder differs by building/solar versus building/drone |
| Marwin | 492.000M | 492.000M | 427.000M radar at each site; remainder differs by site works/solar versus building/drone |

The Udon and Phimai TOR page 4 scans visibly print `LEONARDO/Gematronik` in
the radar specification table and state the same 850-kW magnetron envelope.
Udon page 31 shows the 480M budget, 15% advance payment, 840-day delivery,
and two-year warranty; the corresponding Phimai payment page has not yet been
confirmed and is not treated as identical by inference. These observations
corroborate the dossier's page references; they do not establish that the TOR
was final, that brand equivalence was unlawful, or that any bid was awarded.

The strongest new analytical point is not simply that totals match: the
visible radar-core figures from Scientific (422.770M) and Marwin (427.000M)
are also close at both sites, while the add-on scope is redistributed between
building, solar, road/fence, and drone lines. That may indicate a common
reference envelope, but it is only an investigative lead until the request
letters, original BOQs, Pr.4/Pr.5/Pr.6 forms, and procurement minutes are
obtained.

## Operational-data check

The RRD public readiness API is date-based. A query for 17 September 2567
returned 12 radar records: 9 marked usable and 3 unusable. Phimai was marked
usable with raw data, a 24-hour operating schedule, working network, and
service data. Queries for the corresponding September 2569 dates returned no
data, so this cannot establish the present condition of Phimai or any other
station. It does make the request for current condition reports, uptime logs,
repair history, and an end-of-life notice more precise. The extracted radar
rows are archived in
[rrd_instrument_readiness_radar_extract_2024-09-17.json](data/sources_rrd/rrd_instrument_readiness_radar_extract_2024-09-17.json).

## New source check after the tab sweep

An official RRD five-year action-plan PDF creates a useful timeline question:

- The plan allocated **235M baht for a Phimai S-band dual-polarization radar
  improvement in FY2568**, not 480M.
- The same plan separately listed Phimai infrastructure works in FY2568,
  including truck/car parking, office/meeting-room, restroom, electrical
  system, and radar-operations-building repairs.
- The plan did not list a new Udon Thani fixed radar; it listed a 460M Ban Phue
  purchase in FY2567. The later 480M Udon request therefore appears to be a
  new proposal or a plan change, not a simple execution of the old five-year
  plan.

This does not prove that the 480M replacement is unjustified. It does establish
a precise request: produce the engineering decision, asset-condition report,
and plan-revision record showing when Phimai changed from a 235M improvement to
a 480M replacement. The official plan is [here](https://it.royalrain.go.th/meeting/pdf/660519/p1.pdf),
with the radar entries on page 3.

A historical RRD performance report recorded Phimai radar availability at
100% of the planned 240 operating days in FY2560. That is too old to establish
current condition, but it reinforces why the current EOL, repair history, and
uptime records are necessary before treating the replacement rationale as
settled. [Historical performance report](https://www.royalrain.go.th/UploadFile/11010425630625.pdf)

Finally, a September 2026 parliamentary news report repeats the 960M/2-station
claim and says the speaker counted nine RRD stations and twelve TMD stations.
That station count conflicts with the current RRD official page used in this
repo, which lists twelve RRD stations. The article is useful as evidence of the
public budget challenge, but the count itself should not replace the official
asset register. [Matichon report](https://www.matichon.co.th/politics/news_5880249)

## Primary budget confirmation found after the tab sweep

The Budget Bureau's FY 2570 **draft** expenditure-bill document for the
Ministry of Agriculture and Cooperatives (Document 3, Volume 4) provides the
first primary-source confirmation of the two RRD 480M entries. The document
places both items under RRD's surveying-equipment investment budget:

- **Mueang Mon, Udon Thani:** fixed S-band dual-polarization rain radar,
  72M in FY 2570, with a total commitment of 480M spread across 72M / 192M /
  216M in FY 2570–2572.
- **Rangka Yai, Phimai, Nakhon Ratchasima:** the same 72M first-year amount
  and the same 480M total commitment, also spread 72M / 192M / 216M.

The same RRD section states total FY 2570 expenditure of 3,217,226,300 baht,
including 2,489,374,500 baht in investment expenditure. This changes the
status of the 480M amounts: they are no longer only figures reported by the
ThaiTH.AI dossier; they appear as named, multi-year budget lines in an official
Budget Bureau document. It still does **not** establish that a tender was
awarded, that the full 480M is reasonable, or that either station is needed.

The classification is also informative: the two radar packages are listed as
surveying equipment, while the same document lists other RRD buildings and
construction separately. The budget book therefore confirms the envelope and
cash schedule, but not the line-item BOQ or the radar/building/civil-work split
behind each 480M package.

Because the source is filed under the Budget Bureau's draft bill collection,
we should call it a **draft appropriation entry** until the enacted FY 2570
Budget Act and its annex are linked. The archived PDF and checksum are in
[`data/sources_rrd/budget_draft_2570_agriculture_vol3_4.pdf`](data/sources_rrd/budget_draft_2570_agriculture_vol3_4.pdf).
[Budget Bureau document page](https://www.bb.go.th/topic-detail.php?id=18872&mid=544&catID=1538)

The Budget Bureau's published calendar supports that status: it places the
House consideration on 7–9 September, Senate consideration on 10–11
September, and transmission for promulgation on 21 September 2569. As of this
review date (17 September), we should not describe the two 480M lines as an
enacted appropriation. [Budget Bureau FY2570 calendar](https://www.bb.go.th/index.php?mid=72)
