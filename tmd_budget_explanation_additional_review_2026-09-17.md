## Executive finding

This is a **Thai Meteorological Department (TMD)** budget-explanation document, not a Royal Rainmaking Department (RRD) procurement file. Its 480M-baht item is an **Aviation Hub aviation-weather instrumentation and data-integration package**, not one weather radar. It must not be used as evidence that a single radar costs 480M.

The document adds two useful domestic reference points:

| TMD FY2570 proposal | Scope | Proposed budget | What it can and cannot show |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Aviation Hub | Multi-airport LiDAR, lightning warning, EFM electric-field sensors, software, servers, network/security, UPS and integration | 480M | Confirms a broad multi-site aviation-weather system can reach 480M; not a radar price |
| Ang Thong C-band | 1 C-band dual-polarization Doppler radar, communications link, radar tower and related buildings | 210M | Closest internal turnkey radar benchmark found so far; still a proposed budget, not an awarded price |
| Mobile X-band | 2 dual-polarization X-band radar vehicles with radar, vehicle, power, communications, spares and training | 160M total / 80M each | Useful mobile-radar benchmark; not comparable to a fixed S-band station |
| Buriram AWOS | Replacement automatic weather-observation system at an airport | 50M | Useful replacement/obsolescence documentation benchmark, not a radar benchmark |

## Relevant project details

### 1. Aviation Hub package — 480M

The project is titled “จัดหาครุภัณฑ์วิทยาศาสตร์ รายการเพิ่มประสิทธิภาพเครื่องมืออุตุนิยมวิทยาการบิน เพื่อยกระดับอุตุนิยมวิทยาการบินสู่ Aviation Hub ของประเทศไทย”. It covers eight secondary airports—Chiang Mai, Ubon Ratchathani, Hat Yai, Phuket, Buriram, Nan, Phitsanulok and Hua Hin—and a central aviation-meteorology system at Suvarnabhumi (PDF pp. 3–8; draft TOR pp. 1–8).

The listed scope includes LiDAR wind-shear systems, local lightning-warning systems, atmospheric electric-field sensors, airport and central computers, software, servers/displays, weather-data overlays, WMS, firewalls, switches, UPS, routers and training. The project is scheduled over FY2570–2572 with a 15% advance (72M), 40% first delivery (192M), and 45% final delivery (216M). Delivery is within 720 days and warranty is two years.

The document also says contract signing is conditional on the FY2570 budget law taking effect and the appropriation being received; TMD may cancel if the budget is not allocated. This is therefore a **budget request/explanation**, not proof of procurement, award, or expenditure.

The PDF also embeds three supplier quotations for the Aviation Hub package (PDF pp. 18–20): AsiaMet 480.000M, Marwin 508.176M and Excel Consultants 522.000M. The 480M proposed budget equals the lowest quotation. The quotations are dated 27–30 April 2569 and generally state 720–750-day delivery and one-year warranty. This is primary evidence of a budget-to-lowest-quotation match, but it is for a multi-site aviation package—not for a radar.

### 2. Ang Thong C-band radar — 210M

Project title: “โครงการจัดหาเครื่องเรดาร์ตรวจอากาศ แบบ C Band ชนิด Dual Polarization พร้อมอุปกรณ์เชื่อมโยงและหอเรดาร์ ๑ เครื่อง ที่ สถานีอุตุนิยมวิทยาอ่างทอง (ทดแทน เครื่องเรดาร์ฯ สอต.ชัยนาท)”. It is a new FY2570 project, with 210M committed across FY2570–2571: 136.5M (65%) and 73.5M (35%) (project form PDF pp. 33–49; technical specification pp. 50–55; budget form pp. 43–49).

The package explicitly includes:

- one C-band dual-polarization Doppler radar;
- antenna, pedestal/control, radome, transmitter/receiver and waveguide;
- control, processing and display systems;
- 20-kVA voltage stabilizer, 15-kVA UPS, two 2-kVA UPS units and 50-kVA generator;
- test/maintenance equipment and spares;
- communications/data-link work;
- radar tower, generator building and storage building; and
- training for radar technicians/engineers and control staff.

The stated implementation period is 540 days, with two-year warranty, four preventive-maintenance visits per year during warranty, and a 30-day maximum repair period after written notice. The project narrative describes approximately 240-km operational coverage around Ang Thong, while the equipment list and building scope show this is a turnkey fixed-site package rather than a radar core alone.

### 3. Mobile X-band radar — 160M for two vehicles

Project title: “โครงการจัดหารถเรดาร์ตรวจอากาศเคลื่อนที่รุ่นใหม่ ชนิด Dual Polarization X-Band Radar จำนวน 2 คัน”. The budget is 160M over FY2570–2571: 104M (65%) and 56M (35%) (project form PDF pp. 59–69; technical specification pp. 70–74; budget form pp. 65–68).

Each system includes the radar antenna/control/radome, transmitter/receiver, processing and display, 5-kVA stabilizer, 3-kVA UPS, 6-kVA generator, vehicle and accessories, test/maintenance equipment and other items. The project also includes communications, spares, training and two-year warranty. The attached technical specification states delivery within 540 days; the project-form schedule states 450 days. The payment schedule is 15% advance (24M), 50% first delivery (80M) and 35% final delivery (56M).

The PDF embeds three supplier quotations for this same C-band project (PDF pp. 56–58): Scientific Research 210.000M (24 November 2568), Genomatch 231.200M (20 November 2568), and Marwin 254.650M (21 November 2568). The proposed budget of 210M equals Scientific Research’s lowest quotation. All three quote one C-band dual-polarization radar with link/tower or building scope; the quotations state one-year warranty and delivery terms of 540, 600 and 650 days respectively.

The one-page quotations do not expose model numbers or a line-item BOQ. Marwin’s quotation explicitly identifies the offered brand as EEC (United States); the other two visible pages do not identify a model. This prevents a technical explanation of the 21.26% quote spread without the attached technical offers.

The document justifies the vehicles for areas not covered by fixed stations, backup during fixed-station outages, special missions and local rainfall estimation. That is a different operational model from a fixed S-band radar and should not be used as a direct like-for-like comparison with the RRD Phimai/Udon proposals.

The PDF embeds three supplier quotations for the two-vehicle X-band project (PDF pp. 75–77): Scientific Research 160.000M, Marwin 169.600M and Genomatch 174.400M. The proposed budget of 160M equals Scientific Research’s lowest quotation. The three quoted totals are dated 20–24 November 2568 and state one-year warranty; quoted delivery terms are 540, 650 and 600 days respectively.

These one-page quotations also provide no model numbers or line-item cost build-up, so the unit-price spread cannot yet be attributed to radar hardware, vehicles, software, support or other scope.

Across all three quotation sets, the visible quotations state one-year warranty, while the corresponding TMD project/TOR text requires two-year warranty. This is a material scope/price-normalization issue: the lowest quote may not be economically comparable to the requested package unless the quotation was later clarified or the warranty requirement was changed.

### 4. Buriram AWOS — 50M

The Buriram airport AWOS project is 50M over FY2570–2571. Its stated replacement reasons are particularly useful for the RRD comparison: runway/Touchdown Zone changes, frequent equipment failures, obsolete components and parts no longer manufactured. It specifies two field stations near runways 04 and 22, 450-day delivery, two-year warranty, calibration every six months for four visits, and battery replacement before warranty expiry (PDF pp. 21–32).

This is an example of the level of asset-condition and obsolescence explanation TMD uses when requesting replacement equipment. The RRD Phimai file should be tested against the same questions: failure history, parts discontinuation evidence, repair-versus-replace analysis, expected remaining life, and disposal/redeployment plan for the METEOR 600S.

## Price interpretation for the radar investigation

The 210M Ang Thong proposal is the most relevant internal comparator. It is **not evidence that an RRD S-band radar should cost 210M**, because band, power, range, tower/building design, site conditions, communications, software, spares, training, taxes, importation, warranty and procurement year may differ. It does establish a concrete question for the RRD 480M packages: what additional scope or technical requirement explains an RRD fixed S-band package costing roughly 2.3 times this TMD fixed C-band turnkey proposal?

The comparison should be normalized into at least these buckets:

1. radar core and transmitter/receiver;
2. antenna, pedestal, radome and tower;
3. power systems and generator;
4. buildings, foundations, road/fence and site works;
5. communications and data integration;
6. spares, training, calibration, warranty and post-warranty support; and
7. taxes, freight, installation and other bundled items.

The RRD quotation evidence already shows radar-core indications around 422.770M–427M inside packages near 480M–492M. The TMD document makes that gap more important: the domestic public-sector benchmark for a fixed C-band turnkey radar package is 210M, while RRD's proposed S-band radar core alone appears roughly twice that amount. This is a **high-priority verification lead**, not a finding of overpricing, because the technical and commercial scopes are not yet normalized.

## Document-quality and mapping issues

The Ang Thong project contains an apparent copy/paste inconsistency. The project title and budget repeatedly identify the site as Ang Thong replacing the Chainat radar, but the output-indicator table on PDF p. 45 refers to delivery at the Southern Meteorological Center in Phuket. This should be recorded as a document-control defect and clarified with TMD before relying on the indicator table.

The mobile X-band section has a second timing inconsistency: the project-form schedule states 450 days (PDF p. 64), while the attached technical specification states 540 days (PDF p. 74). The contract timetable and payment milestones should be checked against the signed/final TOR.
